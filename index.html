Lesson 1

How a STAR answer is built

Behavioral questions start with "Tell me about a time when..." The interviewer is using your past behavior to predict how you will perform. STAR keeps your story focused, and adding Lessons shows you grow from experience. Aim for 1 to 2 minutes spoken, about 200 to 300 words.

STACTION 60%RL
SituationSet the scene in one or two sentences. Where, when, what was at stake.
TaskYour responsibility. What you personally owned.
ActionThe longest part. Your steps, decisions and why. Say "I", not "we".
ResultThe outcome with numbers: D0 points, minutes saved, overtime hours, dollars.
LessonsWhat you learned and what you do differently now.
Lesson 2

Five habits that separate strong answers

  • Own it. "We fixed the staffing gap" hides you. "I rebuilt the early shift bid" shows you.
  • Quantify the result. "Things improved" becomes "D0 recovered from 78% to 86% in two weeks."
  • Keep the setup short. Long background eats the time the interviewer needs for your actions.
  • Show your thinking. Explain why you chose each step, not just what you did.
  • Close with a lesson. Especially on failure questions, the lesson is what they remember.

Build 8 to 10 stories covering leadership, conflict, failure, initiative, teamwork, pressure and adaptability. One strong story can answer several questions.
